Keep yourself safe

    
Must-knows

    

      
Always read before you send.Fill in anything in [brackets] and fix anything that sounds off. The draft is a starting point.

      
Use official forms for contracts.The assistant gives you a summary of the terms. You put them on your brokerage's TREC forms. It never writes contract language.

      
Never type wiring details or SSNs.Don't paste bank account numbers, wiring instructions or Social Security numbers into the assistant.

      
Your broker has the final word.The Texas rules it follows are a guide, not legal advice. When something feels unclear, ask your broker.

      
Describe homes, not people.Fair housing law: never say who a home is "perfect for", and don't answer "is it safe?" with an opinion. The assistant helps you with this.

      
Paperwork before showings.In Texas, a buyer signs a written agreement before you show them any home. The assistant reminds you.

    

  


  

    
Questions

    
Common questions

    

      
Do I have to type the exact words on this page?
No. These are examples. Say it however feels natural. If it isn't sure what you mean, it asks.

      
It's asking me a lot of questions. Is that normal?
Yes. It asks instead of guessing, especially about dates, prices and client details. If you don't know an answer, say "I don't know yet" and it leaves a blank to fill later.

      
Can it send emails or texts for me?
No, on purpose. If Gmail is connected it can save a draft in your Gmail, but you press send. It won't draft a text to anyone who hasn't agreed to receive texts.

      
What's in the [brackets]?
Things only you know or need to check, like whether a house is still available, a showing time, or your brokerage's address. Fill them in before sending.

      
Where does it keep my clients' information?
In your real estate folder on the computer, one file per client. You never have to open those files; just ask, for example "What's the latest with the Chens?"

      
What's the "IABS" it keeps mentioning?
Information About Brokerage Services, a one-page Texas form explaining how agents work. You must share it the first time you talk with someone about a specific home. The assistant puts the link in your first email. Your broker gives you the link.

      
Does it update Follow Up Boss for me?
Yes, once it's connected with your Follow Up Boss API key, and only after you click Allow on each change. HAR.com, LoopNet, Crexi and RentSpree don't allow outside apps to log in, so for those you paste their emails or searches. Never give the assistant your passwords for any site.

      
What is "Allow it?" and should I click it?
It appears whenever the assistant wants to change something in Follow Up Boss. Read the one-line summary (for example "update Keisha Brown to stage Appointment Set"). If it's right, click Allow. If not, click Deny and tell the assistant what to fix.

      
How will I know if Texas real estate rules change?
A daily check watches TREC and new Texas laws. If something changes, you get an email in plain English. Once Scott approves the update, it also appears at the top of "What should I do today?", and the assistant starts following the new rule. You can ask any time: "Any new Texas rule changes?" Always confirm details with your broker.

      
Do I need to pay for Claude?
Yes. This runs in Claude Code, which needs a paid Claude plan. Claude Pro (about $20 a month) is enough. Use your own account, not someone else's, so your clients' information stays in your account.

      
Can I change my info later?
Yes. Update the boxes in "Make it yours" and paste the new message, or just say something like "My new phone number is …" or "Make my emails a little more formal."

      
The first time I use it, it asks for my license number. Why?
So every email and ad it writes has your name, your brokerage and your TREC license number on it, as Texas rules require. It only asks once.

      
Something looks wrong. What do I do?
Tell it, in plain words: "That price is wrong, it's $449,000" or "Make this shorter and friendlier." It fixes it. If you're unsure whether something is allowed, ask your broker.
